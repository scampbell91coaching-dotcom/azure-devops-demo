# V7.9 exercise taxonomy audit

## Decision

The 342-record catalogue is adequate as a browsable exercise library and as a
conservative swap foundation. It is **not yet expressive enough to drive Block
Factory, session warm-up recommendations, or athlete movement-need matching
without coach-authored metadata and explicit translation rules**.

No migration is justified. The existing nullable fields can support a reviewed
next catalogue version. The immediate issue is data meaning and population,
not storage capacity. Automatic selection must remain opt-in.

## Scope and method

The generated source, committed JSON asset, importer/model, exercise editor,
swap service, accessory intelligence, Block Factory, warm-up domain, athlete
state and relevant tests were inspected. Counts below are from the schema 7
asset generated by `scripts/build_exercise_catalogue.py`.

The asset has 342 records: 30 squat, 30 bench, 30 deadlift, 200 accessory and
52 warm-up. Canonical-name and alias identities have no collision. The builder
already rejects exact normalised identity duplicates.

## Consumer audit

### Block Factory

`AccessoryRepository.automatic_candidates()` requires `auto_select=true`.
Every production catalogue record omits `auto_select`, `lift_relevance`,
`training_phases` and `compatibility_tags`; imports therefore retain model
defaults/nulls and produce **zero seeded automatic candidates**. This is a safe
opt-in boundary, but means the “automatic” mode only works after a coach edits
records. Once enabled, selection filters phase/lift/compatibility and sorts by
coach priority, fatigue and name. It does not balance movement needs, muscle
coverage, role, total fatigue, equipment availability or athlete constraints.

The role shown in previews is inferred by `_accessory_role()` from broad
purpose/name/family strings. This creates a second, undocumented taxonomy and
can classify by incidental words. The role should become explicit before it
affects quotas or placement.

### Warm-up recommendations

The catalogue and persisted warm-up protocol domain are parallel systems. The
protocol domain has phases, lift scope, athlete/session tags, instructions,
ordering and provenance; the exercise catalogue has names and generic
preparation metadata. There is no canonical link between a protocol step and an
exercise ID and no adapter that ranks the 52 warm-up records. Catalogue coverage
therefore does not currently provide warm-up recommendations.

### Movement-need matching

Technical observations and athlete constraint flags are human-authored text.
Catalogue purposes and constraints use generated tags, with no controlled map
between the two. Weekly intelligence can expose the text as context but cannot
perform exact, explainable exercise matching. Adding more fuzzy name matching
would hide uncertainty and is not recommended.

## Inconsistencies and duplicate concepts

- `movement`, `family`, `category`, `movement_pattern` and `specificity` each
  partly classify exercise type. `category` particularly mixes relationship
  (`variation`, `regression`), laterality (`unilateral`) and sport/use
  (`conditioning`, `gpp`, `strongman`).
- `competition_relevance`, `specificity`, `lift_family`, `lift_relevance` and
  `variation_of` overlap. Recommended authority: `specificity` plus
  `variation_of` for relationship, `lift_relevance` for multi-lift assistance.
- `category=unilateral` duplicated `constraint_tags=unilateral` and had one
  inconsistent record: Kickstand Romanian Deadlift. The safe V7.9 catalogue fix
  adds its missing constraint tag and a regression test.
- `family=Calves and grip` and `movement_pattern=calf_or_grip` merge unrelated
  needs. Similar composites merge press/fly, hip extension/abduction, shoulder
  press/raise and carry/sled.
- `technical_purposes` and Block Factory’s derived accessory role overlap but
  are not equivalent. Grip selection further treats purposes and compatibility
  tags as one namespace.
- `equipment` and `equipment_options` currently duplicate the same statement;
  many “options” are unsplit alternative lists.
- No exact name/alias duplicate was found. Similar-name pairs are plausible
  distinct variations and should not be merged without coach review.

## Safe V7.9 changes

1. Added `kickstand` to the builder’s established unilateral-property rule.
2. Regenerated the schema 7 JSON asset; no schema or migration changed.
3. Added tests that keep the generated asset in sync, require unilateral
   category/tag consistency, and protect canonical competition relationships.

No seeded record was opted into automatic selection. No clinical compatibility,
technical correction, progression edge, grip or strap meaning was invented.

## Recommended order of work

1. Approve controlled vocabularies and field authority, including explicit
   laterality, accessory role, warm-up role, grip/strap semantics and tag
   namespaces.
2. Review and split composite movement patterns and equipment alternatives.
3. Populate accessory lift relevance, role and phase on a small coach-reviewed
   pilot set; only then opt those records into automatic selection.
4. Define lift-scoped technical need-to-feature mappings and a separately
   confirmed athlete-constraint translation layer.
5. Connect warm-up catalogue exercise IDs to protocol steps with dose,
   sequencing and provenance.
6. Evaluate coach accept/reject/override data before broadening automation.

## Acceptance boundary

The catalogue is ready for search, manual selection and conservative same-group
swap suggestions. It is not approved as an autonomous source for accessory
selection, warm-up prescription, technical correction or constraint-based
exercise exclusion.
