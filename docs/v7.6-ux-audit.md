# Traditional Strength V7.6 UX audit

**Audit date:** 10 August 2026  
**Scope:** current repository implementation after V7.4; coach and athlete beta journeys  
**Overall UX score:** **6.8/10**  
**Target after the proposed V7.6 batch:** **8.2–8.5/10 perceived quality**

## Executive finding

The product is functionally credible for a supervised beta. The key money path—invite, activate, publish, train, finish, review—is implemented and covered end to end. Empty states, role protection, responsive breakpoints, status feedback, and basic focus treatment are better than an early beta normally provides.

The remaining quality gap is primarily interaction design, not architecture. Coaches must scan too many equally weighted panels and controls; athletes see several competing “next” destinations; scheduling is explained as a limitation instead of represented as a clear state; and programming exposes domain/storage terminology that is useful to developers but not to a coach making a decision. Mobile tests establish that pages fit, but not that dense editing and reviewing tasks are quick or understandable.

The smallest useful V7.6 batch should therefore tighten action priority, language, navigation context, and mobile ergonomics while leaving routes, data models, authentication architecture, and framework choices intact.

## Method and scoring

This is a code-and-coverage audit, not a moderated usability study. Evidence reviewed:

- Flask routes and presentation services in `platform-portal/portal/`, especially `athletes.py`, `auth.py`, `block_factory.py`, `checkins.py`, `coach_dashboard.py`, `programming_routes/`, and the dashboard/scheduling services.
- Current Jinja templates under `platform-portal/templates/coach`, `athletes`, `programming`, `checkins`, `nutrition`, and `auth`.
- Shared and surface CSS/JS under `platform-portal/static/`.
- Playwright coverage in `e2e/tests/coach.spec.ts`, `athlete-training.spec.ts`, `mobile.spec.ts`, `auth.spec.ts`, and `pilot-money-path.spec.ts`.

Scores are judgement-based: 1 is unusable, 5 is workable with material friction, 8 is confident beta quality, and 10 is exceptionally clear and polished. Each surface score is the mean of clarity (Cl), information hierarchy (IH), primary-action visibility (PA), cognitive load (CLd), consistency (Co), mobile usability (M), error/empty states (EE), trust/polish (TP), and accessibility basics (A11y), rounded to one decimal. The overall score is weighted toward daily coach review and the athlete training path, rather than a simple mean.

### Score evidence by surface

- **Coach dashboard — 7.3.** The action queue is correctly first and uses explicit review links; all dashboard panels have useful empty states (`templates/coach/dashboard.html:17-130`). Four summary counts are visually equal and are not links, however, so they do not accelerate triage. The page grows into seven panels without filtering, urgency sorting controls, or a single resume action.
- **Athlete list/detail — 6.2.** The roster table is scan-friendly on desktop (`templates/athletes/list.html:80-146`), but the eight-field create form precedes the roster (`:17-78`), making the common “open an athlete” task secondary. The detail page combines account lifecycle, imported nutrition, training, nutrition review, profile metrics, and programme access in one long document (`templates/athletes/dashboard.html`).
- **Coach review queue — 7.1.** Weekly, nutrition, and session work converge in one queue with specific CTAs (`templates/coach/dashboard.html:25-51`) and the pilot test verifies a finished session enters it (`e2e/tests/pilot-money-path.spec.ts:136-150`). Review experiences still diverge: check-ins use a detail route, training a log route, and nutrition jumps to an anchor in the long athlete record.
- **Programming hierarchy — 5.9.** Block/session pages have breadcrumbs (`templates/programming/block.html:7-9`, `session.html:19`), but the week page relies on a back link and the athlete-program page’s “Back to athlete” points to the roster, not that athlete (`templates/programming/athlete_program.html:27-32`). Dense CRUD controls and repeated nested panels compete with the main editing task.
- **Block Factory — 5.9.** Preview-before-create is a sound safety model and current E2E tests cover ordering, incomplete state, provenance, validation, and dismissal (`e2e/tests/coach.spec.ts:201-300`). The wizard remains a large, terminology-heavy form whose generated reasoning, incomplete-data warnings, proposal state, and override requirements all have comparable visual weight (`templates/programming/factory.html`).
- **Session builder — 5.4.** Context, autosave status, templates, warm-up, main lifts, assistance, shortcuts, and provenance are all present (`templates/programming/session.html:19-145`), but this produces the highest cognitive load in the product. A ten-column sheet and drag-only reordering are intrinsically weak on a phone; most inputs after “Sets” lack explicit accessible names (`:111-118`).
- **Athlete dashboard — 7.7.** It foregrounds training and uses honest empty states. The next session has a direct CTA and unscheduled state (`templates/athletes/athlete_dashboard.html:21-46`). Five persistent nav choices and several dashboard cards compete; “Today” can display the next session in sequence even when it is not scheduled today.
- **Athlete programme/session — 7.9.** Week/session structure, status, lock explanation, field constraints, sticky save/finish controls, and failure preservation are strong (`templates/athletes/programme.html:10-35`, `programme_session.html:17-71`). The session’s set controls require both “Complete” and “Skip” checkboxes, allowing an ambiguous mutually checked state unless JS/server validation resolves it.
- **Warm-up — 6.2.** Athlete rendering is ordered and readable (`templates/athletes/programme_session.html:23`). Coach authoring exposes a pipe-delimited mini-language, protocol version, assignment reason, override reason, numeric phase values, and three adjacent disclosure widgets (`templates/programming/session.html:51-56`), making a routine task feel administrative.
- **Check-ins — 7.0.** The athlete form groups questions semantically, preserves values, explains scales, and has a clear submit (`templates/checkins/form.html`). The coach list is a separate operational destination while nutrition review lives on athlete detail, weakening the idea of one review inbox. Check-in “due” links can also open an athlete form from a coach context without making impersonation/data-entry ownership explicit.
- **Nutrition — 6.4.** Honest source labels and robust no-data states build trust (`templates/nutrition/index.html:25-120`). Every athlete receives a large card with metrics, bodyweight table, weekly summary, and timestamps, so the dashboard scales poorly and provides no “needs review / stale / on target” sorting or next action.
- **Calendar/scheduling — 5.4.** The application consistently admits when dates are absent, but it repeatedly explains the fallback algorithm (“first unfinished session”, “programme order”) rather than presenting a simple plan state (`templates/programming/athlete_program.html:37`, `templates/athletes/programme.html:15`, `athlete_dashboard.html:38-40`). There is no visible coach scheduling action in the audited hierarchy despite date-aware presentation services.
- **Auth/activation — 8.1.** Login has strong labels, autocomplete, error announcement, password visibility, session-expiry messaging, and explicit 320/390/430 coverage (`templates/auth/login.html:29-84`, `e2e/tests/auth.spec.ts:42-76`). Activation has clear unavailable-link recovery but no show-password control or visible password criteria beyond length (`templates/auth/set_password.html:14-29`). Manual delivery copy exposes transport implementation.

## Visual hierarchy and navigation inconsistencies

1. Coach pages use the dark `coach_workspace.css`; athlete pages use a separate compressed stylesheet imported through `athlete_dashboard.css`; auth uses a third visual system. Separate role themes are reasonable, but button, notice, status, and empty-state semantics should share names and behaviour.
2. The design-system primitives (`templates/design_system/components.html`, `static/css/design-system.css`) are not the primitives used by the core coach/athlete journeys. This encourages slight differences such as `coach-primary-button`, `button`, `auth-submit`, `form-notice`, `auth-notice`, and `nutrition-error-summary`.
3. Coach top navigation contains eight destinations plus Platform and Sign out (`coach/base.html:61-127`). Operations and product tasks have equal prominence; **Platform** is duplicated between desktop actions and the mobile utility group.
4. The coach dashboard correctly starts with review, but four metric cards look actionable without being links (`coach/dashboard.html:17-22`). Below them, urgent health flags and informational nutrition tables use the same panel weight.
5. Programming alternates among cards, nested panels, disclosures, breadcrumbs, back links, spreadsheets, and inline forms. Hierarchy is structurally present but visually noisy.
6. Athlete desktop says **Home** while mobile says **Today** for the same route (`athletes/base.html:18-35`). **Check-in** links to history, whereas the dashboard CTA says **Complete check-in**. **Account** is only an anchor on a long dashboard.
7. Review status language varies: “Requiring review,” “Needs review,” “Awaiting review,” “Pending,” and “No weekly check-in due.” A compact shared vocabulary—**Due**, **Needs review**, **Reviewed**, **Not scheduled**—would improve scanning.
8. Some primary page actions sit inside prose headers (`programming/index.html:5-7`), some in action groups, and others inside disclosures. Use a predictable title + state + one primary action pattern.

## Smallest V7.6 refinement batch

This is one focused batch, estimated at roughly 5–8 implementation days plus review. It deliberately avoids new entities, schedule architecture, broad visual redesign, and framework work.

### 1. Make the next action unmistakable

- Rename athlete dashboard **Today** content to **Next session** whenever no planned date exists; preserve **Today** only for an actual date match.
- Make summary counts on `/coach` link to their corresponding panel anchors and place pain/fatigue immediately after the review queue.
- Put athlete roster before creation; expose **Add athlete** as the header CTA/disclosure.
- Add triage state and a direct review/open action to nutrition athlete cards.

### 2. Simplify coach programming without changing its model

- Standardise breadcrumbs on athlete programme, block, week, and session pages.
- Move template and warm-up management below/core-adjacent to prescriptions and collapse secondary controls.
- Put destructive/lifecycle actions under **More actions** with a danger treatment.
- On mobile, turn the assistance spreadsheet into labelled stacked rows and add keyboard/touch-operable move controls.

### 3. Replace internal copy and unify state language

- Apply the copy replacements above.
- Use **Due / Needs review / Reviewed / Not scheduled / Active / Draft** consistently.
- Keep provenance/source text only where it changes a coach decision or builds data trust; move it to secondary text.

### 4. Close beta-confidence accessibility gaps

- Give every session-builder input an explicit label or `aria-label` including exercise name and field.
- Ensure Complete and Skip behave as mutually exclusive controls with an announced state.
- On validation, focus the error summary and link each summary item to the field.
- Maintain ≥44px touch targets for mobile nav, disclosure summaries, add/delete/move, Save, and Finish.

If implemented as a single batch, these changes improve the most frequently seen screens and the paid beta path without pretending to solve full calendar authoring. The likely result is approximately 8.2/10 in a static re-score; reaching a defensible 8.5 requires one short moderated pass with at least two coaches and three athletes, then fixing any repeated task failure.

## Proposed acceptance tests

Add these to the current Playwright suite using existing deterministic fixtures.

### Coach dashboard and review

1. At 320, 390, and 430 px, `/coach` shows one clearly styled **Review next item** or an explicit queue-clear state without horizontal overflow.
2. Activating each summary metric moves focus to the correctly labelled panel.
3. A completed athlete session appears once in **Needs review**; opening it and returning preserves queue context.
4. A nutrition review link lands on the exact check-in heading, not merely the top of athlete detail.
5. Queue states use only the agreed labels and do not rely on colour alone.

### Athletes and navigation

1. `/athletes` exposes the roster before the create form in DOM and visual order; **Add athlete** expands the form and moves focus to First name.
2. At all three phone widths, a roster item exposes name, status, and a single open action without requiring horizontal table scrolling.
3. Programming breadcrumbs resolve athlete → block → week → session, and the athlete crumb returns to `/athletes/<id>`.
4. Desktop and mobile athlete navigation use the same destination labels and correct `aria-current` state.

### Scheduling language

1. With no planned date, athlete home displays **Next session**, **Not scheduled**, and no claim that it is today.
2. With a planned date equal to today, it displays **Today** and the formatted date.
3. With a future date, it displays **Scheduled for [date]**.
4. No user-visible string contains “first unfinished,” “status cannot be calculated,” or “programme order” on these paths.

### Programming/session builder

1. At 320/390/430 px, every assistance field has an accessible name containing its exercise and attribute; no row requires horizontal scrolling.
2. Keyboard users can add, edit, move, and delete an assistance exercise; saved status transitions from Saving to Saved and is announced politely.
3. Template, warm-up, and destructive actions are initially secondary/collapsed; opening them does not move or obscure the primary prescription controls.
4. Delete/archive actions have danger styling and require a confirmation naming the affected entity; cancel preserves it.
5. Warm-up steps render in the same order and with equivalent dosage on coach preview and athlete session at all phone widths.

### Athlete session/check-ins/nutrition

1. Selecting **Complete** clears **Skip**, and selecting **Skip** clears **Complete**; the submitted value survives reload.
2. Invalid session and check-in submission focuses a linked error summary while retaining entered values.
3. Sticky Save/Finish actions do not cover the final input and remain ≥44px tall at 320/390/430 px.
4. Nutrition overview can identify and open a needs-review athlete using keyboard alone; its status has text, not colour alone.

### Auth/account activation

1. Activation and reset pages provide show/hide password controls, announce mismatch/length errors, retain focus logically, and fit 320/390/430 px.
2. Successful invitation copy says where it was sent and what the coach should do next; it does not mention “transactional email service” or claim delivery beyond known state.
3. Invalid/expired/revoked/used links retain the current recovery CTA to sign in/contact the coach.

## Coverage gaps to retain in the release checklist

- Current `mobile.spec.ts` checks headings, nav visibility, control presence, and `scrollWidth`, but not text clipping, sticky-action occlusion, tap target size, zoom/reflow, focus order, or landscape.
- Block Factory tests validate generation correctness more deeply than wizard comprehension or recovery from invalid input.
- There is no automated accessibility scan and no evidence here of screen-reader testing.
- Tables use overflow wrappers, which prevents page overflow but does not make roster/nutrition comparison effective at 320 px.
- No test measures task time or whether a coach can correctly answer “who needs me first?”; moderated beta observation is required to validate the 8.5 target.

## Release recommendation

Proceed with a V7.6 UX refinement release after the four-part batch and acceptance tests above. Do not bundle a scheduling engine, auth rewrite, component-framework migration, or visual rebrand. The current foundation is sufficient; perceived quality will move most by reducing choices, clarifying state, and making the supervised beta’s next action obvious.
