# V7.6 Block Factory coaching refinement

## Scope and decisions

V7.6 extends the V7.2 accessory-intelligence seam rather than adding another
exercise model. Automatic candidates still come only from active,
accessory-suitable catalogue records explicitly enabled by a coach.

Accessory volume is deterministic and means a fatigue-unit budget for automatic
suggestions per training day:

- Low: 3 fatigue units
- Medium: 6 fatigue units
- High: 9 fatigue units

The policy has no accessory-count ceiling. Candidates are considered in the
stable coach-priority, fatigue, and name order supplied by AccessoryIntelligence.
Each eligible exercise consumes its catalogue fatigue rating, clamped to the
documented 1-5 range; an item that does not fit is skipped. Thus the default
fatigue rating of 3 preserves the prior 1/2/3 output, while fatigue-1 work can
produce 3/6/9 accessories where the enabled, compatible catalogue justifies it.
The available catalogue may produce fewer, including zero. `No assistance`
always produces zero. Any manually added accessory switches the request to
manual mode; those choices replace all automatic suggestions, retain coach
order, and are distributed once across the training days. Manual selections do
not consume the automatic budget and have no count ceiling.

Eligibility remains exact and metadata-led: active, accessory-suitable and
coach-enabled rows must match phase, lift relevance, requested compatibility,
constraints and exclusions. The policy makes no medical or injury inference.
Every generated item retains its reasons and provenance and adds the fatigue
cost/budget reason used for selection. Runtime selection is ordinary Python and
database logic; no LLM participates.

## Deadlift grip context

The factory captures intended competition grip (`Hook`, `Mixed`,
`Double-overhand`, `Straps`, or `Other`), grip-work priority (`None`, `Maintain`,
`Build`, or `Priority`), and training strap usage (`None`, `Some work sets`, or
`Most work sets`). Strap usage is separate so a coach can describe strapped
training while retaining a different intended competition grip.

On deadlift days, a non-`None` priority promotes at most the available
grip-relevant catalogue candidates within the normal accessory-volume maximum.
Recognition prefers structured technical-purpose/compatibility metadata and
supports conservative legacy names for static bar holds, double-overhand holds,
hook-grip practice/tolerance, and controlled no-strap deadlift exposure. Loaded
carries are explicitly excluded from the grip shortlist; grip programming is not
implemented as generic farmer-carry selection.

Every automatic choice retains the existing catalogue reasons and adds the
matched grip family, priority, competition grip, and (when applicable) separate
strap-usage rationale. Reasons are visible in preview. This context is coaching
input, not an injury inference or a claim that every athlete needs grip work.

## Persistence and assumptions

No migration is introduced. The controls round-trip in the signed Block Factory
proposal JSON and therefore govern preview and acceptance safely. They are not
persisted as reusable athlete defaults or queryable block columns after the
proposal lifecycle. Persisting long-lived athlete grip preferences or block
intent would require schema design and a migration; that work intentionally stops
at the current domain/UI seam.

`Straps` remains an allowed competition-grip value because it is a stated product
requirement, while training strap usage remains independently represented.
Catalogue coverage may initially be sparse because automatic selection remains
opt-in. Coaches can still pin any suitable catalogue accessory manually.

## Coverage

Focused Python tests cover deterministic volume, manual override, zero
assistance, grip context and rationale, strap separation, and loaded-carry
exclusion. Browser coverage verifies all controls round-trip and that `No
assistance` overrides high volume and priority grip work.
