# V7.9 programme corpus parity

## Decision

The repository does **not** currently contain an imported real-programme corpus.
There is no programme workbook, CSV, JSON programme export, SQL snapshot, or
version-controlled production database in the current tree or its Git history.
The only bundled JSON called `traditional_strength_intelligence.json` is a
342-row exercise catalogue: its top-level keys are `schema_version`,
`catalogue`, `language`, and `exercises`; every `occurrences` value is zero. It
does not contain blocks, weeks, sessions, or prescriptions.

Consequently, V7.9 must not claim that the current automatic accessory maxima
have been validated against real coaching programmes. The maintained evidence
has no session with more than three accessories and therefore gives no basis to
raise High above 3. The deterministic parity policy is to retain Low/Medium/High
at 1/2/3 until an attributable imported corpus demonstrates otherwise, while
preserving coach pins and explicit `No assistance` as authoritative exceptions.

## Measured maintained structure

### Accessories

Across the ten canonical maintained sessions, 8 have zero accessories and 2
have one. None has 2, 3, or more than 3. The maximum is 1 and the mean is 0.2.

Exposure counts overlap for combined sessions:

| Exposure | Sessions | Accessory distribution | Mean accessories |
| --- | ---: | --- | ---: |
| Squat | 7 | 5 × zero, 2 × one | 0.29 |
| Bench | 5 | 4 × zero, 1 × one | 0.20 |
| Deadlift | 4 | 3 × zero, 1 × one | 0.25 |

The only repeated accessory family is upper pulling: `Squat Named Row` and
`Cable Row`, two occurrences. That is too little evidence to infer a rotation,
quota, or lift-specific family policy.

### Sessions above three accessories

**None found.** This applies to all version-controlled imported, seeded,
fixture, and historical sources found in the inventory. Generated test cases
exercise a maximum of three automatic accessories but contain no observed
session above three. If a future import yields such sessions, the report must
list stable corpus/session identifiers rather than only an aggregate.

### Lift slots and prescriptions

Counting primary plus secondary lift work, slot totals are 1 in 5 sessions, 2
in 3 sessions, and 3 in 2 sessions. Nine sessions contain primary/top work only.
The pilot E2E session is the sole secondary/back-off example: squat top work
`1×3 @ RPE 7.5–8.5`, pause-squat back-off work `2×5 @ RPE 6.5–7.5`, then cable
row `2×10 @ RPE 6–7`.

Other maintained patterns are deliberately small:

- Static S/D prescriptions: `1×3 @ 7`.
- Static bench: `1×5 @ 7` alone, `1×4 @ 7` in combined templates.
- Stable E2E top rows: `3×5 @ 7`.

Block Factory itself supports broader generated phase patterns, but these are
implementation policy rather than corpus observations. Hypertrophy,
development, strength, peaking, and offseason map to main/accessory reps of
8/10, 5/8, 3/6, 1/4, and 6/10 respectively. RPE progresses linearly through
configured ranges (6–7.5, 6–8, 6.5–8.5, 7–9, 6–7.5); non-peaking final weeks
drop one RPE point. First-position sets are 3 except strength (4) and peaking
(1); later rows receive 3 sets through position 3 and 2 thereafter.

### Session distribution and competition evidence

Classifying bench-only as upper, single squat/deadlift exposure as lower, and
multi-family sessions as full-body gives 1 upper, 5 lower, and 4 full-body
sessions. This is a transparent reporting convention, not stored corpus
metadata.

There is no evidence-backed phase comparison. Static templates use competition
lifts, one E2E block says “strength”, and one objective mentions competition
strength, but no imported sessions carry comparable offseason/development/
peaking labels. Factory goal mappings must not be described as observed
competition-phase differences.

## Comparison with current 1/2/3 maxima

| Factory volume | Automatic maximum per session | Sessions in maintained corpus at that count | Evidence conclusion |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Low | 1 | 2 | Can represent the observed maximum |
| Medium | 2 | 0 | Not contradicted; not corpus-validated |
| High | 3 | 0 | Not contradicted; no evidence to increase it |

Automatic maxima are ceilings, not targets. Catalogue availability and
deterministic filtering may produce fewer. Manual mode is intentionally outside
these automatic ceilings: pins replace suggestions, retain coach order, and are
distributed once across days. Explicit `No assistance` always means zero,
including when High or grip priority is selected.

## Deterministic parity policy

1. Treat only attributable source/import sessions as corpus observations.
   Generated Factory previews cannot validate Factory policy.
2. Retain automatic maxima `{low: 1, medium: 2, high: 3}` while the count of
   observed corpus sessions above three is zero.
3. Re-evaluate High only after a versioned import contains at least one session
   above three. Publish the count, denominator, phase/exposures, and stable
   session identifiers. Do not infer a new cap from catalogue size.
4. If re-evaluation is triggered, choose the smallest deterministic ceiling
   covering the approved target percentile, with that percentile agreed before
   production code changes. Outliers remain coach-pin territory by default.
5. Never truncate, reorder, supplement, or replace manual pins to satisfy an
   automatic ceiling. Manual order remains authoritative.
6. `No assistance` is an explicit zero, never missing data or permission to
   auto-fill.
7. Unknown phase/body classification remains unknown. Do not manufacture
   competition-phase differences from block names or Factory mappings.

This policy makes the next decision reproducible while acknowledging that
genuine parity remains blocked on importing real programmes with provenance.

## Golden regression coverage

`test_programme_corpus_parity.py` recalculates the fixture distributions,
identifies sessions above three, checks the static-template transcription
against production template data, and locks the authority rules. Existing
`test_block_factory_v3.py` tests remain the executable coverage for automatic
1/2/3 output, manual replacement/order, and explicit zero assistance.
